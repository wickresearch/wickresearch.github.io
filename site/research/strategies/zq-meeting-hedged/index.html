Trading the Shape of the Fed Funds Curve Around FOMC Meetings

A basket of consecutive 30-Day Fed Funds futures, weighted so that every scheduled FOMC decision inside it cancels, leaves a small deviation that reverts; the strategy supplies liquidity against it.

How the basket cancels the decision

A ZQ contract settles at 100 minus the average daily effective fed funds rate (EFFR) over its calendar month. If the FOMC moves the rate on day d of an N-day month, that month’s average is a mix of the old and new rates with weights d/N and (N−d)/N, the weights Kuttner (2001) uses to extract policy surprises. The month before sits wholly at the old rate and the month after wholly at the new one, so lots in the ratio below have zero exposure to both rates, whatever the Fed does.

before : meeting : after = d : −N : (N−d)

With a second decision in a neighbouring month, the basket extends to four or five legs and the condition becomes F′x = 0, where F holds each month’s share of days in each rate regime. The lots sum to zero, so a parallel shift in expected EFFR also cancels. The rate level is hedged with the same contract at other expiries, with no basis to another market.

Worked example, Sep-2026. Decisions on 16 September (d = 16, N = 30) and 28 October (d = 28, N = 31); legs August to November. Exact ratios +0.5333, −1, +0.5167, −0.0500. Whole lots +1 −2 +1 +0 leave at most 0.0968 lots exposed to any regime rate, so a 25bp decision moves one unit by at most 0.0968 × $41.67 × 25 = $100.81, against $362 for the average winning trade.

Why the basket moves at all. Hedgers and dealers trade single months, which pushes one month away from its neighbours; futures carry time-varying premia (Piazzesi and Swanson, 2008); EFFR moves against the Fed’s administered rates and has calendar effects (Bech and Klee, 2011; Hamilton, 1996); settlements sit on a 0.5bp grid. The rules forecast nothing about rates: the trade is paid for taking the other side of single-month flow.

Evidence

1-minute backtest

The hedge holds in the P&L

Daily P&L regressed on the change in the rate level: $8.56 per bp (t = 0.6). On the change in curve slope: −$27.09 per bp (t = −1.1). R² of both 0.01.

The eight FOMC decision days averaged $134, against $122 on other days (p = 0.96). With eight days the test has little power; it is consistent with the hedge rather than proof of it.

1-minute backtest

The result does not rest on a few trades

Mean trade $173, median $121, t = 5.1. Without the five best trades, net P&L is $22,691. The 95% bootstrap interval for the Sharpe ratio is 0.9 to 4.9.

Re-run at other fees, with thresholds recomputed: $45,346 at $2.00, $28,924 at $3.00 and $11,396 at $4.00 per contract round trip (Sharpe 1.28). Break-even is about $5.28.

Settlement-based reconstruction (model)

2010–2025, under the same rules

Only daily settlements exist before 2026, so the same rules were run on them using relationships measured on the 1-minute year. Net P&L per year of structures: mean $32,970 (s.e. $4,862), median $26,575, range $17,043 to $85,655; positive in 16 of 16 years.

This is model-based, not a record of fills. Because it reproduces the average behaviour of a profitable year, positive years are largely true by construction. It is informative about how the calendar changes outcomes, not as a forecast.

Where the cost drag sits

Fees took 52% of gross profit in the 1-minute backtest ($34,424 of $66,047). Fees scale with every contract in a unit, while gross profit scales with the quarter-month lots, so a structure that needs many contracts to hedge pays far more per unit of edge.

The three four-contract structures made $20,780 of the $31,622. A 22-contract structure traded the most (72 trades) and kept $1,820, with fees at 88% of its gross. Which structures a year offers is set by the FOMC calendar: a decision near mid-month gives the simple 1 : −2 : 1. In the reconstruction, the share of trades in small structures has a rank correlation of 0.60 with yearly net P&L, and the two best years, 2015 and 2018, had 72% and 79% of trades in four-contract structures.

Limitations and capacity

  • One year of 1-minute data: 183 trades in one rate regime. The rules were designed on the same year, and the 2010–2025 figures extrapolate it.
  • Fills are at the mid. The net edge is $1.26 per contract fill, about 6% of a $20.835 tick, so execution, including completing every leg (Kozhan and Tham, 2012), decides whether the result can be captured.
  • Capacity is set by volume in the thinnest deferred leg, not by capital. Peak participation on entries was 8.3% of a leg’s daily volume; peak margin was $74,177.
  • The margin netting credit is an assumption (tested at 70% and 85%), and position sizing is unresolved.
